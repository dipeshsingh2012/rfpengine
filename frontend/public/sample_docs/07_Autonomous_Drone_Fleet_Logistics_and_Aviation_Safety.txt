TITAN AEROSPACE & AUTONOMOUS LOGISTICS
GLOBAL FLEET OPERATIONS AND AVIATION SAFETY STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE (SOP)
Document Reference: SOP-2026-AERO-TITAN-V4
Classification: Restricted / Vendor Operational Policy

1. Drone Battery Safety, Thermal Runaway Mitigation, and Fast-Charging
All autonomous cargo delivery drones in the Titan Fleet utilize custom Lithium-Iron-Phosphate (LiFePO4) battery packs equipped with solid-state ceramic separator coatings. Charging is conducted via proprietary Gallium Nitride (GaN) 800W automated ground pads. Battery operating temperatures are continuously monitored across 16 internal thermistors; the nominal operating range is 15°C to 45°C. If any cell exceeds 55°C during flight, the avionics computer initiates an immediate auto-land sequence at the nearest pre-designated emergency landing waypoint. Battery packs reaching 800 charge cycles or exhibiting greater than 8% internal resistance degradation are retired automatically from flight operations.

2. FAA Part 107 and Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) Regulatory Compliance
Titan Aerospace operates commercial drone delivery services under Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) BVLOS Waiver Certificate FAA-W-2026-TITAN-09. All aircraft maintain strict flight envelopes with a maximum operational cruise altitude of 380 feet Above Ground Level (AGL), ensuring a mandatory 120-foot buffer beneath the FAA 500-foot ceiling for uncontrolled Class G airspace. Collision avoidance is powered by a 360-degree dual Solid-State LiDAR system combined with dual-band ADS-B In/Out transponders operating simultaneously on 978 MHz UAT and 1090 MHz Extended Squitter frequencies.

3. Autonomous Geofencing, Fail-Safe Protocols, and Ballistic Parachutes
Every Titan delivery drone is equipped with a pyrotechnic ballistic parachute recovery system capable of full deployment in under 120 milliseconds upon sensor detection of catastrophic motor failure, structural vibration exceeding 8.5G, or pitch/roll deviations exceeding 65 degrees. Dynamic geofencing firmware enforces a mandatory 5-nautical-mile exclusion perimeter around Class B, C, and D airports, military installations, and real-time active wildfire temporary flight restriction (TFR) zones. In the event of total command-and-control (C2) link loss exceeding 4.5 seconds, the drone immediately ascends 30 feet to clear local obstacles, halts forward flight, and executes an autonomous Return-to-Home (RTH) track using multi-constellation GPS/Galileo/BeiDou GNSS guidance.

4. Pilot-in-Command (PIC) Remote Teleoperation and Network Latency SLA
Remote teleoperation and fleet oversight are governed by certified FAA Part 107 Remote Pilots-in-Command (PIC) stationed at Regional Operations Centers (ROCs). Under normal autonomous flight operations, a single PIC may supervise a maximum ratio of 12 active aircraft simultaneously. Telemetry, video streaming, and manual override commands are transmitted over redundant private 5G mmWave cellular networks with secondary Low-Earth Orbit (LEO) Starlink satellite failover. The end-to-end command latency SLA is guaranteed at less than 25 milliseconds round-trip time (RTT). If end-to-end network latency exceeds 85 milliseconds for more than 2 consecutive seconds, supervisory control automatically switches to the secondary satellite telemetry link.

5. Cargo Handling, Hazardous Materials Restrictions, and Secure Payload Release
Titan cargo bays support a maximum payload capacity of 6.5 kg (14.3 lbs) housed within weatherproof carbon-fiber capsules. Transport of Class 1 explosives, radioactive materials, and volatile chemicals is strictly prohibited. Medical cargo, including temperature-sensitive vaccines and emergency blood units, is maintained within active thermal insulation pods keeping internal temperatures between 2°C and 8°C for up to 4 hours. Payload delivery is executed using a motorized high-tensile Spectra tether system with automated magnetic ground-release; the aircraft remains hovering at 25 feet AGL without landing during customer drop-off.
